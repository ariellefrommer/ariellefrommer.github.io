About Me

I'm an incoming Ph.D. student in Astronomy at Yale University and an NSF Graduate Research Fellow and Yale Gruber Science Fellow. My research focuses on exoplanets — worlds orbiting other stars — using observations and computational tools to understand how planets form, why they vary across different systems, and whether any might harbor life.

Most recently, I was an Alex G. Booth Fellow at Leiden Observatory in the Netherlands. I received my B.S. in Astrophysics and Physics from Harvard University in May 2025, graduating cum laude with highest honors in Astronomy.

I'm also passionate about science communication! Since April 2024, I've published 36 articles for Sky & Telescope covering the latest discoveries in astronomy and astrophysics. I've also created educational tours on art and astronomy at the Harvard Art Museums.

Research

I study exoplanets — worlds orbiting other stars — with a focus on atmospheric characterization and planetary formation.

Exoplanet Atmospheres · JWST

Constraining Carbon Monoxide Isotopes in WASP-39b

Awarded Harvard's competitive Alex G. Booth Fellowship to spend eight months as a researcher at Leiden Observatory, advised by Prof. Ignas Snellen. Aiming to measure carbon monoxide isotopes in the JWST transmission spectrum of hot Jupiter WASP-39b, a novel measurement which could probe the planet's formation history.

  • Funded by Alex G. Booth Fellowship

Exoplanet Detection · Machine Learning

Using Machine Learning to Find More Planets

Updating systematic corrections on Transiting Exoplanet Survey Satellite (TESS) light curves using machine learning, with the goal of improving systematic noise corrections and increasing sensitivity to transit detection. Advised by Dr. Jason Eastman and Prof. Andrew Vanderburg.
